## What it meant

**"Fifth Tiger" died within a fortnight, and the people who killed it were Thai.**
The phrase always contained an apology — *fifth*, behind Korea, Taiwan, Singapore,
Hong Kong. By April the columnists were openly irritated by it. Nobody in Seoul
reformed a police force. Nobody in Singapore had to.

**The deep state is what people could not stop talking about.** For fifty years the
honest description of Thai political economy was a protection economy: the same
organisation investigated and arrested, so enforcement was a revenue stream; the
bureaucracy was a patronage payroll before it was an administration. This cabinet
uncoupled arrest from investigation, gave prosecutors a service that could act
without a police referral, and put the early-retirement programme through with
weeks left. **Execution hit 90%** — budget that used to evaporate between
appropriation and delivery now arrives.

The mood among people who deal with the state daily was not gratitude. It was
disorientation. *"It just… worked?"*

**The diaspora turned around.** The 86,000-engineer pipeline stopped being an
aspirational document. Returning became a defensible career decision for the first
time since the nineties.

**The young stopped believing the ceiling exists.** They are not grateful — People's
still holds 97 seats — but the ceiling was the thing, and it went.

### The three cold facts
1. Output gap **+5.39%**. Every Thai boom has ended the same way.
2. Capital stock grew **2.1%**. Almost nothing ordered has been built. The country
   is celebrating a pipeline.
3. In Ranong and Chumphon the land was taken from people who could not stop it,
   by a government that then won an election on the corridor.

### And the number nobody could explain
**Approval finished at 52.8%.** Not adoration. Just over half the country, mildly
approving, of the most consequential administration since Thai Rak Thai.

*"The most striking thing is not what it achieved but how ordinary the country
found it."*
