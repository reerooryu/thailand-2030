### Bhumjaithai + Pheu Thai + Others · 2026Q2 – 2030Q1

scoreboard the player is judged on and the scoreboard the electorate uses
are not the same document.

## What actually happened

Everything of consequence was decided in the first five quarters. Digital
government and the Super Licence in Q1, the whole revenue architecture —
VAT phased, the Revenue Mobilisation Package, the Negative Income Tax —
across Q3 and Q4, civil service reform in Q5. Pax Silica signed in Q3 and
the data-centre transmission guarantee in Q5, which is what carried the FDI
term and, through it, the investment rate.

The Land Bridge was deferred six separate times and finally pushed in Q14,
whereupon the southern provinces erupted and it was converted into the
Chumphon–Ranong rail link in Q16 — four years of holding a decision open
and then losing it anyway, which is the most realistic sequence in the log.

The 14th National Plan was funded in full in Q8, which unlocked the SMR
programme the same quarter, and the semiconductor package landed in Q12.
Neither delivers anything before 2037.

## Why it won

The output gap finished at **+3.75%**, which is the tell. This was an economy
running hot on public capital at 9.27% of GDP — voters felt 3.28% growth and
watched the index run 67%, and neither of those is the same thing as the
2.81% potential growth the term is scored on. The record term in the election
model converts exactly this: visible boom plus 66% approval.

The fiscal position was the one genuinely defensible thing about it. 76.1%
inside a 78% ceiling with the risk premium held to 0.33pp, after raising the
ceiling once. Spent, not hoarded, and stopped before the market repriced it.

## Why it is not a great term

Potential growth of 2.81% on a reform stock of 63 is *improved*, not
*transformed*. A −5.41% primary balance at the end of an expansion with a
positive output gap is a structural deficit, not stabilisation. And capital
stock growth of 2.0% over four years says most of the megaproject pipeline
had not yet delivered when the votes were counted.

The second term inherits the bill for all three, and now has 302 seats and
no excuse.
